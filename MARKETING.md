# Trayage website and organic content plan

Local review version · October 12, 2026 · Nothing in this plan authorizes publication.

## Message and first audience

**Find the files in Downloads you no longer need.**

Trayage flags installers, possible duplicate downloads, and large or older files in one review. Start with those suggestions instead of checking every file in Downloads. You choose what moves to Trash.

The October 12 copywriter and BNC feedback replaces the abstract homepage tagline with a concrete benefit. “Give Downloads a second look” remains a supporting brand line. The homepage explains the difference from manual Finder cleanup, names the file suggestions, and gives visitors a free first-review step. The near-term objective is more people trying the app and giving useful feedback. There is no established customer evidence to support popularity, satisfaction, or conversion claims.

Speak to the task: managing everyday downloads, finishing a project, or wanting a clearer starting point. Less confident Mac users—including some older users—are an audience to test, not a demographic to stereotype. Use ordinary words, legible screenshots, and a calm pace. Do not claim the app is accessible or easy for everyone before observing actual use.

## What the local website now supports

- Homepage: local visual direction, concrete benefit headline, real screenshot, trial details, plain-language file categories, review workflow, ordinary-week and project examples, a compact pricing strip after the file categories and workflow, a secondary comparison link in the footer, category-aware recent blog posts, FAQs, and first-review feedback invitation.
- `/compare/`: “Why Trayage?” explains what its suggestions add to manual Finder review, with an illustrative file example, clear limits, and an early free-review invitation. No competitor recommendations or outbound sales links.
- `/blog/your-first-trayage-review/`: what choosing a folder, inspecting a suggestion, and confirming cleanup actually mean; trial timing and recovery limits explained.
- `/blog/weekly-downloads-review/`: a modest recurring review routine for ordinary files and client work, with links into the established guides.
- Existing guides: manual Downloads review, DMG decisions, and checking possible duplicates. These remain useful even for someone who does not buy Trayage.
- Blog category archive, RSS, author links, related reading, inline article navigation, and contextual download links.
- Each public article and the comparison has its own 1200 × 630 social preview. No new social accounts or unfinished video placeholders are displayed.
- Copy-link, email, and X sharing are initiated by the visitor. No post is sent automatically.

Homepage selection uses the newest two public posts and the newest remaining post from a different category when available. With one category only, it fills from the next newest post. Draft and noindex posts are excluded. Labels come from content rather than assuming every post is a guide.

## Measure understanding, not only clicks

The starting learning target from the marketing discussion is 10 people completing a review and five feedback conversations. This is a planning target, not a claim about current users.

Track weekly: organic search queries and page visits, article-to-download intent, actual download-link clicks, comparison visits, support questions, and voluntary reports of a completed review. The website cannot infer installation, activation, or completion of an in-app review from a download click.

Existing Tinylytics instrumentation is extended with `download.intent`, `content.compare`, `content.share`, and `feedback.review`. Event values are general labels. No visitor IDs, filenames from a user’s Mac, license keys, or email content are added. These are attempted website actions, not confirmed app outcomes. Confirm actual reporting in the existing account after an approved release.

In feedback conversations ask: What did you expect the app to do? What did you think would happen to selected files? Where did you pause? Were you able to complete one review? Ask before recording personal details. Do not add in-app tracking to solve this website measurement problem.

### Product website voice

Use a product-led voice throughout the website: “Trayage” for product behavior and “you” for the customer’s actions and benefits. Avoid developer narration such as “I’m working on,” “my app,” and “tell me.” Use “Contact support” for customer help. Keep factual developer and publisher credits on the About page, article bylines, media materials, and legal disclosures. Reader-perspective FAQ questions can use “I” or “my.” Do not invent a team by mechanically replacing “I” with “we.”
