Trayage has a new look: violet colors, a new tray icon, and a redesigned app overview. The website is changing with it, bringing the app itself closer to the explanation of what it does.

The feedback behind these changes raised a useful question: can someone arriving for the first time understand Trayage quickly enough to want to try it?

## Start with what the app helps you do

“Give Downloads a second look” captures the idea behind Trayage. But a short line needs a plain explanation beside it.

Trayage helps you find downloads you no longer need. It groups installers, possible duplicate downloads, and large or older files, with a reason to check each suggestion. You review them and choose what moves to Trash.

That explanation matters as much as the colors. A visitor should be able to understand what the app does, what remains their decision, and what they can try for free.

## A violet identity, from the icon to the app

The earlier warm orange and olive direction has made way for violet, pale lavender, and deep plum. The new tray icon carries that palette into the Dock, the app, and the website.

Light and dark appearances belong to the same design. Pale surfaces and darker purple accents work together in light mode; deep plum surfaces and lighter violet accents carry the same hierarchy in dark mode. The aim is a consistent, recognizable app wherever you encounter it.

## Show more of the actual product

Feedback on Buildhop welcomed the purple redesign and suggested giving product screenshots more space. The same review pointed out that the “Before you try Trayage” section felt heavy.

That prompted a closer look at the order of information on the website. A main overview gives visitors a sense of the app, while separate views show the full review window and the smaller menu-bar experiences. Short descriptions explain what each view is useful for.

A quick glance from the menu bar and a longer review in a full window are different ways to use Trayage. Seeing both helps make that choice tangible before downloading.

## Keep the walkthrough, make it optional

Product images give you something to inspect at your own pace. The walkthrough adds something else: seeing a file reviewed and the confirmation opened in the actual app.

The website now puts the product views before pricing, with the walkthrough further down for anyone who wants a closer look. Common questions sit in a shorter, expandable section.

The goal is to make the first visit easier to follow while keeping the detail available. Feedback guided the changes; whether they help people understand Trayage better is something to keep checking, rather than assume from a new design alone.
